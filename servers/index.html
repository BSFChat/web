How servers work

What joining a server means for your privacy

Your BSFChat ID is your account: your username, password, two-step sign-in and the list of servers you have joined, so your other devices can find them. It does not see your messages.

A server is where the conversation lives: its channels, messages, files and calls. Every server is run by someone — us at chat.bsfchat.com, or anyone else who runs the software. Joining one means trusting whoever runs it.

What a server learns when you join

  • An identifier for your BSFChat ID, your display name and your profile picture.
  • Not your password and not your email address. The app never sends either.
  • Your IP address, as with any website you connect to.
  • Everything you post there: messages, files, reactions, and who you call.

What the people who run it can do

  • Read your messages and files on their server. BSFChat messages are not end-to-end encrypted.
  • Keep or delete what is stored there on their own terms. Their rules apply, not ours, except on the servers we run, which follow our privacy policy.
  • Remove you from the server.

Only join servers you trust. If you are unsure, ask whoever runs it how they handle your data.

What stays on your device

  • A sign-in for each server you have joined.
  • Your BSFChat ID sign-in, kept in your system's secure credential store (Keychain on Apple devices, Credential Manager on Windows, the Secret Service on Linux, the Android Keystore on Android) and only on this device. If your system has no secure store, the app keeps it only until you quit.

Leaving

Removing a server in the app signs this device out of it and takes it off your BSFChat ID's server list. What you already posted stays on that server unless you delete it there.
