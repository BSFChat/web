BSFChat is a small project. There is no support desk and no ticket queue — there is a person, an issue tracker, and an email address. This page says which one to use.

First: which server are you on?

BSFChat is self-hosted. The app is one thing; the server your messages actually live on is another, and it is very often run by somebody who is not us — a friend, a guild, a company, or you.

  • Problems with the app — it crashes, a button does nothing, voice will not connect, the UI is broken on your phone — come to us. Use the links below.
  • Problems with the people or the content on a server — someone is harassing you, a channel is full of junk, you want somebody removed — go to whoever runs that server. They are the only person who can act. We cannot moderate a server we do not operate, and in most cases we cannot even see it.
  • Problems on chat.bsfchat.com — the public server we run — come to us, because there we are the admin.

You can see which server you are connected to in the app: it is the server name at the top of the server list.

Safety: blocking, reporting, and leaving

Every BSFChat client has these built in. You do not need to email anybody to use them.

Block someone

Open the member list or tap the person's avatar next to one of their messages, then choose Block user. Once blocked, their messages stop appearing for you, in every channel and in direct messages, on every device you sign in on. You can undo it from the same menu, or from the blocked-users list in your settings.

Report a message or a user

Open the menu on the message itself (long-press on mobile, or the "…" on desktop) and choose Report message. You can add a reason. The report goes to the administrators of the server you are on — the people who can actually do something about it — and not to anyone else.

Leave or delete

Leaving a server is in the server's own menu. Deleting your account entirely is in Settings → Account → Delete account. Read the deletion section of the privacy policy first: deleting your account does not delete the messages you have already sent, and that is a deliberate decision we explain there.

Common problems

"I cannot sign in"

Check the server address first — it is the most common cause. If you are signing in with a BSFChat identity account and it keeps bouncing, sign out completely (Settings → Account → Log out) and add the server again; that resets the stored token, which is usually what has gone stale.

"Voice does not work"

Check that the app has microphone permission in your OS settings. If you can connect but nobody can hear you, check that you are not muted and that the right input device is selected in Settings → Voice.

On iPhone, voice and video are not available at all yet. The iOS app is text-only for now. Voice works on macOS, Windows, Linux and Android.

"I am not getting notifications on my iPhone"

You will not. The iOS app has no push notifications yet — it only shows you new messages while it is open. This is on the roadmap.

"Echo / people hear themselves"

Use headphones. Acoustic echo cancellation is not implemented everywhere yet, so an open speaker and an open microphone in the same room will feed back.

Running your own server

Setup instructions, the reference docker-compose.yml and the deployment notes are in github.com/BSFChat/server. Self-hosting questions are best asked as a GitHub issue rather than by email, so the answer helps the next person too.
